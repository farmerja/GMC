ELECTRON GMC: STATUS, DESIGN AND HOW TO RUN
===========================================
Last updated 2026-11-04. Run every GMC command from the GMC root (/home/aubrey/PycharmProjects/GMC) unless stated
otherwise.


1. OVERVIEW
-----------
A GMC cell model replaces analog Monte Carlo inside one cell. Given a particle entering a cell, it samples what
leaves. A cell transit now has a discrete part and a continuous part:

    discrete    exit class: which face the primary leaves through, or "absorbed" (stopped inside)
                [new] number of secondary electrons that escape the cell (0..10, top class = "10 or more")
    continuous  state on the exit face: position along the face, exit angle, energy, path length

The discrete part is sampled by a classifier. The continuous part is sampled by a flow-matching model that is told
which face was chosen. Cells are then chained: a particle leaving one cell enters the neighbour through the
opposite face.

Pipeline for one cell transit (what the chaining code does):
    1. rotate the entry state into the canonical frame (particle entering through the left face, x = 0)
    2. exit classifier         -> exit class (face or absorbed)       [ml/exit_classifier.py]
       (or the joint classifier -> exit class + secondary count     [ml/exit_secondary_classifier.py], not chained yet)
    3. face-conditioned flow   -> position along that face, angle, energy, path   (exits only)
    4. deposit E_in - E_out in the cell (E_in if absorbed)
    5. rotate back and move the particle to the neighbouring cell


2. WHAT WORKS
-------------
- Face-only exit classifier (ml/exit_classifier.py)
    Calibrated against held-out MC (exit fractions within noise, worst energy/angle bin <= ~2.5 sigma).

- Face-conditioned flow (ml/train.py --data_dim 2d_electron_face)
    Compared with the original 2d_electron flow on single 1 um Cu cells (500k held-out histories):
    3x better exit angle, better path length and exit-class fractions. The original flow sends 2.5% of "exiting"
    electrons back into the cell and has a dip in the straight-through direction (a wrap point in its angle
    encoding). The face flow avoids both by construction.

- Chaining (validation/chain_core.py, validation/gmc_kernels.py, validation/Macro_field.py)
    20 x 20 grid of 1 um Cu cells, 1-100 keV beam, dose error vs the MC-chained reference:
        face flow 1.7% | original flow 2.2% | MC-vs-MC noise floor 1.4%
    Following only the primary (secondary energy deposited where the primary loses it) costs about 1% of total
    dose at these energies.

- [NEW] Joint face + secondary-electron classifier (ml/exit_secondary_classifier.py)
    5 exit classes x 11 count classes (n = 0..10) = 55 classes. Tested on 500k unseen 1 um histories: count
    distribution, P(n >= k) tails, mean count vs energy and exit fractions are all within ~3 sigma of MC.
    A synthetic test confirmed all 55 classes train and sample, including "10 or more".
    Separate file and separate checkpoint (exit_secondary_classifier.pth). It refuses to overwrite
    exit_classifier.pth, and each loader rejects the other's checkpoints.


3. WHAT DOES NOT WORK YET
------------------------
- Secondaries are not in the chain. The joint classifier only says how many secondaries leave. Still needed:
    a. a secondary model: exit face, state and energy of each secondary, with energy as a share of the primary's
       energy loss so energy is conserved;
    b. the primary flow conditioned on the secondary count;
    c. a growable particle stack in validation/chain_core.chain;
    d. an MC-chained reference that includes secondaries (extend MCKernel in validation/mc_chain.py). Check this
       matches the full-grid MC before training any model.
- High-energy dose shortfall: both flows under-deposit by up to ~6% at the top of the energy range because they
  under-fill the rare large-energy-loss tail (hard knock-on collisions). The log energy-loss option
  (--energy_encoding log_loss) did not fix it. To try next: more training data, and conditioning on the secondary
  count.
- Joint classifier at high counts: only tested on 1 um / keV data (max 3 secondaries per primary). The 80-100 GeV,
  1 mm datasets have been regenerated (*_v2, section 7) but the classifier is not trained on them yet. Even there
  escaping counts are low: mean 0.15, P(n >= 4) = 0.2%, P(n >= 10) = 0.02% (~70 of 300k primaries).
- Analog MC: photons are banked at birth, not transported. There is no pair production and no positrons. The
  combined face + electron + photon classifier waits until these are added.
- Only 2D square cells are wired end to end. cell_topology.py already covers quads, triangles, boxes and tetrahedra
  (class numbering and face coordinates), but no data, flows or chaining exist for them yet.


4. HOW THINGS ARE HANDLED
-------------------------
- Class numbering (ml/cell_topology.py): faces first, then interior outcomes.
    2D polygons: face k is the edge from vertex k to vertex k+1, counter-clockwise.
    Square: 0 bottom, 1 right, 2 top, 3 left, 4 absorbed.
    MC status codes (1 left, 2 right, 3 bottom, 4 top, 5 absorbed) are converted by classes_from_status.
    Time-dependent problems will add a "census" class: topology.with_interior("absorbed", "census").
    Meshes mixing triangles and quads can share one classifier using valid_class_mask.
- Classifiers take exactly the flow's input vector, so one normalized tensor feeds both models. They are trained
  with plain cross-entropy (no class reweighting) so the probabilities are calibrated, then temperature-scaled.
  Always SAMPLE from them; never take the most likely class.
  The joint classifier starts each class at its training frequency (prior_bias=True) so counts never seen in
  training keep near-zero probability.
- Secondary count = escaping secondary electrons of all generations, from electron_bank rows with generation > 0,
  grouped by parent. Secondaries that stop inside the cell are dose, not counted. The loader adds n_sec_e and
  E_sec_e columns.
- Face-conditioned flow target: (s, psi, r, energy, path)
    s    position along the face, 0..1
    psi  exit angle from the outward normal (always outgoing)
    r    in-plane direction magnitude, which encodes |w|; the sign of w is drawn at random by symmetry
- Validation references (validation/mc_chain.py):
    full MC     analog MC on the whole grid; the true answer
    MC-chained  the analog MC used as the cell model inside the same chain loop; the best any GMC with this
                structure can do
    GMC vs MC-chained = model error.   MC-chained vs full MC = cost of following only the primary.


5. GOTCHAS
----------
- analog_tp_cu_1um_20k_42.npz (Oct 28) predates the current analog physics (17.7% vs 26.6% backscatter). Do not
  train on it. Use analog_tp_cu_1um_200k_42_current.npz or analog_tp_cu_1um_1M_42.npz.
- material_data.build_library defaults to E_max = 1e2 MeV (100 MeV). Pass E_max=1e5 for GeV runs; the h5 data
  goes to 100 GeV.
- Secondary stack overflow (fixed 2026-11-04 in analog_stepper): in the 2D (infinite-z) geometry, high-energy
  electrons moving almost along z can travel cm to m of material inside one cell. run_transport now pauses the
  current electron when the stack is 75% full and follows the waiting secondaries first (suspend_fraction), so the
  default stack_size=4096 is enough at 100 GeV / 1 mm. Still read any "[!] Overflows" line: overflowed
  secondaries are deposited locally, so the energy balance closes and check_bank_complete does not catch it.
  The old (pre-v2) GeV files were overflowed, but their cell-exit statistics agree with v2 within noise.
- Banks and segment tables now grow automatically (run_transport auto_grow=True: one exact-size, bit-identical
  rerun on overflow; default segment_factor 20). At GeV they are still large (~140 tracked electrons, ~80 KB per
  primary), and run_transport raises MemoryError past 80% of RAM: use ElectronTransport/generate_analog_batches.py.
- Relevance (ElectronTransport/gmc_segments.py): see section 7b for the definition and how the models use it.
  Old segment parts can be re-cut from their raw tables with build_segments.
- The GMC venv has no h5py, so validation/mc_chain.py must run with the ElectronTransport venv. The ElectronTransport
  venv has no torch, so GMC training and chains run with the GMC venv.
- ml/train.py --run_tag suffixes model, result and norm-param paths so a new run does not overwrite the defaults.
  Without it, a 2d_electron run overwrites ml/models/2d_electron_boundary and the default norm params.
- validation/Macro_field.py reads the architecture from the checkpoint; --hidden_dim/--depth/--expansion_factor are
  accepted but ignored.
- Training run-to-run variance is about as large as some of the differences between models. Use 2-3 training
  seeds before trusting small differences.


6. HOW TO RUN (keV Cu example, 1 um cells)
------------------------------------------
Step 0. Generate analog MC data (ElectronTransport venv, from /home/aubrey/PycharmProjects/ElectronTransport).
  AnalogMC.py's __main__ does this (edit N, random_seed, OUTPUT_FILE). Minimal script:

    import numpy as np
    from AnalogMC import run_simulation
    from analog_stepper import VoxelGeometry
    from material_data import Material, build_library
    H5 = 'copper_mc_database.h5'
    lib = build_library([Material.from_element(H5, 29)], H5)          # add E_max=1e5 for GeV
    geo = VoxelGeometry.uniform(1e-4, 1e-4, 50, 50, material=lib.index("Copper"))   # 1 um cell
    d, _, _ = run_simulation(lib, geo, N=1000000, E_in_low=1e-3, E_in_high=1e-1, E_cutoff=1e-3,
                             v_spread=1, benchmark_mode=False, random_seed=42, w_max_angle_deg=90.0)
    np.savez_compressed("analog_tp_cu_1um_1M_42.npz", **d)

  Make an independent test file the same way with another seed (e.g. 7). About 0.4 s per 50k histories at keV.
  For GeV runs use generate_analog_batches.py (defaults are the 80-100 GeV, 1 mm Cu setup), e.g.
      python generate_analog_batches.py --N 300000 --seed 42 --out analog_tp_cu_1mm_80-100GeV_300k_42_v2
  It writes a merged <out>.npz (same keys as run_simulation minus segment tables; GMC loaders read it directly)
  and <out>_segments/part_XXX.npz (full per-batch datasets with segment tables, local indices + primary_offset).

Step 1. Face-only exit classifier (GMC venv)
    python ml/exit_classifier.py --data_dim 2d_electron \
        --electron_npz ../ElectronTransport/analog_tp_cu_1um_200k_42_current.npz \
        --out ml/models/2d_electron_analog_cu/exit_classifier.pth
  Writes exit_classifier_check_*.png next to the checkpoint (predicted vs MC class fractions per bin).

Step 2. Joint face + secondary-count classifier
    python ml/exit_secondary_classifier.py \
        --electron_npz ../ElectronTransport/analog_tp_cu_1um_1M_42.npz \

        --out ml/models/2d_electron_analog_cu/exit_secondary_classifier.pth   [--max_secondaries 10]
  Writes exit_secondary_check_val.png / _test.png and prints P(n >= k), exit-class and mean-count z-scores.

Step 3. Flows

                         --electron_npz ../ElectronTransport/analog_tp_cu_1um_200k_42_current.npz --run_tag _analog_cu

                         --electron_npz ../ElectronTransport/analog_tp_cu_1um_200k_42_current.npz --run_tag _analog_cu

  Outputs: ml/models/2d_electron[_face]_boundary_analog_cu/best_model.pth and

        --test_npz ../ElectronTransport/analog_tp_cu_1um_500k_7.npz \

7b.1 Relevant secondaries
  A tracked secondary electron (above the 1 keV cutoff) is RELEVANT to the cell it is born in if it, or any of its
  descendants, leaves that cell. Every other secondary, together with its whole subtree, stays in the cell. The GMC
  never simulates those: their energy is dose of the transit that made them.
  - Computed exactly from the analog MC family trees (gmc_segments.relevance), at every depth. In practice two
    levels catch everything: a secondary that escapes itself, or one that is absorbed but has a daughter that
    escapes (about 1 in 600 relevant secondaries in the 100 um data; one 3-level case in the 2M test primaries).
  - Photons. The 100 um data were cut with photons_relevant=False (generate_analog_batches.py
    --photons_irrelevant): emitting a photon does NOT make an electron relevant, because photons are not
    transported. Photon energy simply leaves, as in the analog MC, which banks photons without transporting them.
    The photon energy from irrelevant subtrees is charged to the transit that made them (segment column E_sub_ph).
    The default photons_relevant=True is for when photon transport exists: then a subtree that emits a photon
    must be simulated so the photon can be handed to photon transport.
  - Why it matters: at 100 um only about 0.5 % of tracked secondaries are relevant (about 0.02 per primary,
    rarely more than 2 in one transit). The GMC follows those few instead of all of them, and the rest is exact dose.
  - Relevant secondaries are a BIASED sample: they are known to have an escaping subtree, so they leave far more
    often than a typical electron born at the same point. They must not be run through a model trained on all
    electrons, which would absorb most of them and lose the escapes they were selected for. Hence the separate
    interior models below, trained on relevant secondaries only.

7b.2 What one transit samples
  An electron either ENTERS a cell through a face (a primary, or any electron crossing from a neighbour) or is a
  relevant secondary STARTING at its birth point inside the cell. Each case has its own classifier and exit flow.
  1. Joint classifier: exit class x number of relevant daughters x photon flag (7b.3).
  2. Exit flow (exits only): position along the exit face s, angle from the outward normal psi, in-plane magnitude
     r (which encodes |w|) and the energy-loss fraction lambda = (E0 - E_out) / (E0 - E_cut). It is conditioned on
     the classifier's face (one-hot), n / 3 and the photon flag, so the exit state matches the sampled branch.
     Absorbed: E_out = 0.
  3. Daughter flow, once per relevant daughter j = 0 .. n-1: birth point, direction (w relative to the parent's
     w sign) and the energy share f_j of the budget B_j, with B_0 = E0 - E_out and B_{j+1} = B_j - E_j.
     Shared by entry and interior parents (a start-type input tells them apart).
  4. Photon flow (only if the photon flag is set): photon energy q as a share of what is left, B_n.
  5. dose = B_n - q. Energy is conserved exactly in every transit by construction.
  Each relevant daughter becomes an interior particle in the same cell (step 1 again with the interior models).
  An electron that leaves a cell, primary or secondary, enters the neighbour as a fresh entry: relevance is per
  cell, so in the new cell it is unconditioned and uses the entry models.

7b.3 The joint classifier in detail
  Classes (40): label = (exit * 4 + min(n, 3)) * 2 + photon_flag   (SecondaryGMC.spec.encode / decode)
    exit         0 bottom, 1 right, 2 top, 3 left, 4 absorbed. Faces are numbered CCW as in cell_topology; in the
                 entry models' canonical frame the electron enters through face 3 (left).
    n            relevant DIRECT daughters made in this transit (segment column n_rel_e), capped at 3. The top class
                 means "3 or more"; at most 3 daughters are then generated, and the energy of any 4th stays in the
                 budget and becomes dose. 4 or more occurred in 6 of 10.2M training transits and 1 of 2.04M test
                 transits. Grandchildren are not counted here:
                 a relevant daughter's own relevant daughters come from its own interior transit.
    photon_flag  1 if any photon energy leaves in this transit: q = E0 - E_out - E_rel - E_dep_rel > 1e-12 E0.
                 This counts the transit's own photons plus those of its irrelevant subtrees. About 6 % of transits.
                 The flag is a class rather than part of the photon flow so that the 94 % of transits with q = 0
                 exactly are reproduced exactly; a flow cannot represent a point mass at zero.
  The three are sampled JOINTLY, so their correlations are kept: for example, transits that make a relevant
  daughter lose more energy and exit differently.
  Inputs (in [-1, 1]; the same vector conditions the exit flow):
    entry     (clf_B): 2y - 1 (entry point along the left face), cos phi, sin phi, 2r - 1, e
    interior  (clf_I): 2x - 1, 2y - 1, cos phi, sin phi, 2r - 1, e
    phi = in-plane direction angle, r = sqrt(u^2 + v^2) (so |w| = sqrt(1 - r^2); the sign of w is irrelevant to the
    x-y transport), e = log-energy scaled between the 1 keV cutoff and 10 MeV.
  Training data (ElectronTransport/analog_cu_100um_1keV-10MeV_log_10M_42.npz, --simulated_only):
    clf_B  every primary's transit: 10M rows; 39 of the 40 classes occur.
    clf_I  every relevant secondary's first transit (origin = born, generation > 0): about 200k rows, x 8 by the
           square's symmetries (4 rotations x reflection). The positions, directions and exit face are transformed
           together, so the exit class is relabelled consistently. 27 classes occur. "absorbed with n = 0" is
           impossible here: a relevant secondary that stops must have a relevant daughter. The model learns
           near-zero probability for it.
  Network and fit: ml/exit_classifier.ExitClassifier (MLP, 256 hidden, 4 residual blocks), trained with
  train_exit_classifier: plain cross-entropy with no class reweighting (so probabilities stay calibrated), output
  bias started at the log class frequencies (prior_bias, so rare and unseen classes start near zero), AdamW +
  OneCycle, 15 epochs, batch 16384, 10 % validation split. A temperature is then fitted on the validation split
  (T = 1.00 for clf_B). Sampling draws from the probabilities, never the most likely class, with a seeded
  generator (SecondaryGMC seed).
  Checks (validation/results/secondary_cell_cu_100um, 2M held-out primaries, seed 7):
    exit-class fractions within 2 sigma in every energy bin, for both classifiers;
    P(>= 1 relevant daughter) within 4.2 sigma (entry) and 3.1 sigma (interior); worst at 10 MeV, 5 % low;
    relevant daughters made: GMC 9840 vs MC 10080 (entry), 308 vs 359 (interior);
    photon energy leaving vs E matches (boundary_rates_vs_E.png).

7b.4 What is NOT modelled
  - Splits (K = 10 relevant daughters in one transit, models B->I / I->I): none occur at 100 um. The 10 M training
    primaries contain zero. They are needed for thick (cm), high-energy cells.
  - Interior electrons that are NOT known to be relevant (photo- or Compton electrons once photons are transported,
    volume sources) need unconditioned interior models, trained on an interior source
    (AnalogMC source="interior"); clf_I must not be used for them.
  - One cell size and one material per model set.

7b.5 Results (20 x 20 chain, 4 GMC runs each)
  At 1-10 MeV the dose-map error vs full analog MC is 1.18 +- 0.10 % with secondaries and 3.76 +- 0.24 % without,
  against a 1.1 % noise floor. The MC-chained version with secondaries reproduces the full MC (1.06 %). Run-to-run
  spread is about 0.1-0.2 %, so compare models over several seeds (--n_seeds).
